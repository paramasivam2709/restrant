The Art of the Perfect Tandoor

Clay, charcoal and patience — inside our signature smoke.

Home/Blog Details
Kenji Rao — Sous Chef, Grills Feb 24, 2026 6 min read

There is a moment, about forty minutes after the charcoal goes in, when the tandoor stops being an oven and starts being an instrument. The clay walls glow the colour of the evening sun and the air inside does something you can hear — a low, even hum that tells you the heat has settled into its working rhythm.

1. The Clay Knows Best

Our tandoors are hand-built in Rajasthan from river clay mixed with grass fibre. Each one seasons differently: the first hundred skewers are never served to guests. They are the tuition fee the clay charges for learning how to hold heat evenly.

2. Marinade Is Architecture

Yogurt carries the spice; mustard oil carries the yogurt; time carries everything else. A minimum of eight hours lets the marinade penetrate to the bone — anything less and you're merely painting the surface.

3. Respect the Sides

The front of the skewer cooks faster than the back, so every ninety seconds we rotate — not for colour, but because heat that sits still burns. The char you see on our tandoori chicken is the record of a hundred small attentions.

"You don't command a tandoor. You negotiate with it — every service, every skewer."
— Chef Kenji Rao

Next time you visit, ask for a seat near the grill counter. Watch the rotation rhythm, listen for the hum. That's fifteen years of negotiation you can taste.
